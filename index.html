CostTooling

A phone app for tracking a job from the site: photos, progress, costs, schedule, and the one-page report that comes out of them. This is a working prototype with one sample job. It's built for a phone, so open it there if you can, but everything works here too.

How to help: tap around. Whenever you think "I wish it did…", tap the yellow bulb in the corner and write it down. Ideas save on your device; "Email all ideas" sends them to us.

See every screen at a glance →

Screens

  1. Projects — the list she lands on, with the three numbers that matter per job.
  2. New project — name, client, dates, contract, and a template of line items to start from.
  3. Project home — percent complete, days left, cost so far, cost to finish, the four things she does most, and a mini schedule.
  4. Site log — dated photo entries with a note, conditions and the line item they belong to. "Add photo" opens the camera.
  5. Photo detail — one entry full-size, with "use on cover".
  6. Cover page maker — pick photos, choose a layout; the numbers come from the schedule or she types them. Save to Photos really works.
  7. Progress — one slider per line item, with a Schedule toggle for the Gantt and finish forecast.
  8. Line item detail — budget, dates, the costs logged against it, and its photos.
  9. Report — the one page she shows someone: numbers, summary, table, photos, note.

The idea underneath

Everything is a view of one project record: line items (budget, percent complete, costs, planned dates) and log entries (photo, date, note, conditions, line item). The cover page, the schedule and the report are three ways of showing the same data, which is why moving a slider on Progress changes the cover page too.

Things we'd love your take on
  1. Is percent complete per line item how you think about progress, or is it tasks and dollars separately?
  2. Should "cost so far" come from receipts and invoices you log, or is earned value enough?
  3. Which photos belong on a cover page: the latest, or ones you pick?
  4. Who sees your reports most often, and what do they ask you?
