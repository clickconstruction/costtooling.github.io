How to check a contractor's bill against the work done

The bill says framing is 100% complete. The owner asks if they should pay it. You can answer that with one site walk and a few photos.

What the bill is telling you

A contractor's bill lists each part of the job, such as framing, roofing or plumbing. Each part has a dollar amount. Beside it is a percent.

That percent is how much of the part the contractor says is done. The owner is asked to pay for that much of it.

You may hear other names for the same paper. Some call it a pay application. A lender may call it a draw request. The list of parts is often called a schedule of values.

Four steps on one site walk

1. Know the parts before you go

Read the bill first. Write down each part it lists. Those are the things you will look at.

2. Photograph each part

Walk the job and take a photo of every part on the list. Keep each photo with the part it shows and the day you took it. Add a short note on what you saw.

3. Say how far along each part is

You do not have to be a builder. Five plain answers cover it: not started, just started, about half, nearly done, or done.

If a part looks nearly done, say what is still open. "Blocking for the kitchen uppers is still open" is a useful note.

4. Put what you saw beside what was billed

Go down the bill one part at a time. Compare the percent billed with what you saw. Where the two agree, there is nothing to do. Where the bill is higher, you have a question to ask.

What "billed ahead" means

A part is billed ahead when the bill asks for more of it than is built. Here is one part of a sample job.

$22,800Framing
100%billed
85%on site
$3,420billed ahead

The bill asks for all $22,800. The work on site is worth 85% of that, which is $19,380. The difference is $3,420.

That does not mean anyone did something wrong. It means the owner can ask about that one part before paying for it.

Two things that are not a problem

Materials on site that are not put in yet. A contractor may bill for materials that were delivered and are waiting to be used. Many bills show these in their own column. Check that the materials are there, and count them apart from finished work.

Small differences. Your percent is a judgement, and so is the contractor's. A few points either way is normal. Look for the part where the gap is large.

What to send the owner

The owner does not need the whole bill explained. One page is enough:

  • How much of the job is done, and how much is billed.
  • Each part that is billed ahead, in one sentence.
  • A few dated photos that show it.
  • Your note on what happens next.
